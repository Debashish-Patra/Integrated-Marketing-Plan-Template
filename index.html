An integrated marketing plan, section by section

Thirteen sections that cover every facet of integrated marketing. Each has a table to fill in, a worked example from an investing platform and from an airline, and quality checks to pass before sign-off.

How to use it

Copy the sections into a new document for each engagement. Fill the plan on a page first, then complete sections 1 to 13 in order. Text in [brackets] is a prompt to replace. Ranges and percentages are starting points to adjust, not rules.

The same two businesses run through every section, so you can see how one template gives different answers: the investing platform is limited by conversion after the click and dependence on one channel, while the airline is limited by a low share of direct bookings.

Investing platformIllustrative example

  • Spend scaled 40% with no other change, and cost per first-trade customer rose 45%.
  • One search platform delivered about 80% of paid results.
  • Push reach was 40%, and 12-month retention was 17%.
  • Binding constraint: conversion after the click, made worse by concentration in one channel.

AirlineIllustrative example

  • 62% of bookings came through agents and metasearch.
  • Brand searches leaked to agent ads, and the booking flow lost 75% of people who started.
  • Only 22% of travellers enrolled in the loyalty programme at booking.
  • Binding constraint: direct share of bookings is too low.
Quality checks
  • Every claim cites a source and the numbers share one period and definition.
  • The constraint is one thing, not a list.
  • Competitor data is current.

Investing platformIllustrative example

  • Business: first-trade customers from 5,300 to 7,900 a month in 12 months.
  • Efficiency: cost per first-trade customer within 20% of ₹11,250, so no more than ₹13,500.
  • Retention: 12-month retention from 17% to 25%.
  • Guardrails: invalid leads under 10%; pause if cost per customer stays above tolerance for a full cycle.

AirlineIllustrative example

  • Business: direct share of bookings from 38% to 45% in 12 months.
  • Efficiency: cost per direct booking within 10% of $67, so no more than $73.
  • Loyalty: enrolment at booking from 22% to 35%.
  • Guardrails: average fare not diluted by discounting; pause on the same numeric trigger.
Quality checks
  • Every target has a baseline, a number and a date.
  • Targets add up to the business goal.
  • No target relies on a promise another team has not accepted.

Investing platformIllustrative example

  • Now: active self-directed investors (large, high value, heavy competition).
  • Next: mutual-fund holders ready to move into direct investing.
  • Later, cheaper treatment: beginners, served through education content and in-app onboarding rather than paid acquisition.

AirlineIllustrative example

  • Now: business travellers on the top ten routes (high value, high frequency).

4. Positioning and brand

Define one clear place in the customer’s mind and the proof that earns it.

Fill in

Investing platformIllustrative example

  • Statement: for active investors who want clarity before they commit, the platform shows charges, margin and execution quality on every order, unlike apps that reveal costs afterwards.
  • Claim: orders execute fast. Evidence: published latency benchmark. Owner: engineering. If it fails: a visible status page and a message to affected users within minutes.

AirlineIllustrative example

  • Statement: for business travellers who value time, the airline makes the trip run on your schedule, unlike carriers that charge for flexibility, because changes are free up to two hours before departure.
  • Claim: reliable and flexible. Evidence: on-time record and fare rules. Owner: operations and revenue management. If it fails: automatic rebooking offered in the app within ten minutes.
Quality checks
  • The statement names a real alternative.
  • Each pillar has proof.
  • Each claim has an owner who agreed to it.
  • The brand can show the claim in the product, not only in advertising.

Rules: no platform above about 35% of paid spend; no campaign above about half of acquisition; launch in waves (foundation, scale, extend); start one flagship content series first.

Investing platformIllustrative example

  • Capture: search on account-opening and stock-name terms.
  • Create: an educational video series as the flagship, plus SEO guides.
  • Convert: retargeting by stage (installed but not funded, funded but not traded).
  • Extend: referral and fintech-content partnerships as the second source, and meetups for frequent traders.
  • Retain: push, email and WhatsApp journeys once reach is fixed.

AirlineIllustrative example

  • Capture: defend brand search and add route-level search.
  • Create: outdoor, press and online video in origin cities for route launches.
  • Convert: metasearch with direct fare placement; sequential retargeting after a search.
  • Extend: co-branded card and hotel partnerships; agent portal and corporate desk kits.
  • Retain: pre-trip, in-trip and post-trip CRM to loyalty members.
Quality checks
  • Each facet is planned or deliberately left out, with a reason.
  • One calendar, one message system, shared audiences.
  • Every channel has one owner and one measure.

Final review checklist

Use this before the plan goes for approval.

  • The diagnosis names one binding constraint, backed by data.
  • Every objective has a baseline, target, date and owner.
  • No more than three priority segments, each sized and valued.
  • The positioning statement names a real alternative and has proof.
  • Each claim has an accountable owner outside marketing and a failure plan.
  • Messages are mapped to segment and funnel stage.
  • Every integrated facet is planned or left out with a reason.
  • No platform exceeds about 35% of paid spend, or a second source is planned.
  • The budget reconciles, with no double counting or misclassified lines.
  • Measurement, data and lifecycle have funded lines and owners.
  • The funnel model has low, base and high scenarios with stated assumptions.
  • The KPI tree has definitions, baselines and targets.
  • Governance, risk register and compliance gates are in place.
  • A 90-day roadmap with gates and a funded test reserve exist.
  • The plan fits on one page and a new reader can explain it in two minutes.
